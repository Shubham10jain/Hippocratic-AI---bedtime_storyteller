## Pipeline (read-aloud script)

The Quilt That Went Missing

In a little green cottage at the edge of Thistledown Meadow lived an old grandmother named Nell. She owned a patchwork quilt that smelled of lavender and toast. Every night she tucked it snugly around her sleepy hedgehog, Bramble, who grumbled a little, then hiccuped softly as he settled in.

[softly] "Hic," said Bramble. "Goodnight, Nell."

One evening, Bramble wandered into the front room and stopped short. The basket by the fire was empty. The quilt was gone.

[voice: grumpy hedgehog] "Oh, prickles!" he huffed. "A hedgehog cannot sleep properly without something warm to curl into. I must find it before the moon rises."

So he searched. He peered under the woodpile. He poked his nose inside the teapot. [pause] He found a sock. He found a spool of thread. And he found a very surprised mouse, who squeaked and scurried off with its whiskers twitching.

[slowly] No quilt.

Bramble sat down on the front step with a hiccup and a long, slow sigh. The meadow was quiet. Somewhere, a cricket was tuning up for the night.

Then Bramble noticed something in the moonlight. A trail of tiny muddy paw prints led past the garden gate and down the path toward the pond.

"Hmm," said Bramble, rubbing his chin. He remembered the young fox cubs from the far hill. They liked to borrow warm things, but they always gave them back. "Chasing them would only make them run," he said. "I will go and ask them kindly."

[whisper] So Bramble crept along the reeds, one careful step at a time. The reeds rustled. The water lapped. And there on the bank sat three fox cubs, wrapped up in the patchwork quilt, their ears twitching at every sound.

Bramble cleared his throat. "Hello," he said gently. "That quilt belongs to Nell. It is needed back home."

The smallest cub looked down at her paws. "We're sorry," she whispered. "We only wanted something soft. We're scared of the dark, and the reeds make such creaky noises."

[softly] Bramble thought for a moment. Then his prickles lifted with an idea. "I know a trick," he said. "Watch."

He picked up an old jam jar lying in the grass and gently tucked a sleepy firefly inside. It glowed gold and warm, like a tiny lantern. The cubs leaned in close, their eyes wide.

[together: Soft and slow, the lantern glow.]

The cubs shuffled over and made room. The four of them sat together on the bank, the quilt around all their shoulders, and watched the fireflies blink on and off like little stars. Bramble opened the jar, and the firefly floated away over the water, and another one came to take its place.

When the cubs had yawned three times each, Bramble folded the quilt neatly over his arm. It was heavy, but cosy.

"Thank you," he said. "Come and visit us soon."

The cubs waved from the reeds. The pond went quiet and silver under the moon.

[slowly] Bramble walked all the way home, very slowly, so the quilt would not slip. He tucked it back into its basket by the fire. Then he climbed in, curled up tight, and felt the lavender-scented warmth wrap around him.

[yawn] Nell came in with her lamp, smiled down at the basket, and pulled the edges up under his chin.

"Hic," said Bramble sleepily. "Goodnight, Nell."

[together: Soft and slow, the lantern glow.]

## Baseline (original single call)

Once upon a time, in a quiet village nestled between rolling hills, there lived a little owl named Pippin. Pippin had soft gray feathers, big golden eyes, and a very important job: every night, when the moon rose over the sleepy valley, she would fly from tree to tree and gently tell the sleeping creatures that everything was safe.

One chilly evening, Pippin noticed that the moon looked a little lonely. It was hanging high in the sky, but no stars were keeping it company.

"Oh dear," Pippin hooted softly. "The stars must be tired tonight."

So she flew up, up, up, past the highest branches and the tallest hills, until she reached the edge of the sky. There she found the stars curled up in a cloud, yawning sleepily.

"Why aren't you shining?" Pippin asked.

The smallest star, who was barely bigger than a firefly, blinked and whispered, "I'm afraid of the dark. It's so big, and I'm so little."

Pippin fluffed her feathers and settled down beside the little star. "The dark isn't scary," she said. "It's just a blanket that helps everyone rest. And you don't have to be big to shine. You only have to be yourself."

The little star thought about this for a long, quiet moment. Then, slowly, it began to glow. Soft and silver, it lit up the sky, and one by one, the other stars woke up and joined in, until the whole sky sparkled like a field of diamonds.

The moon smiled, the valley glowed, and Pippin flew home through the silver night, feeling warm and happy.

And as the stars twinkled above, all the creatures of the village drifted off to sleep, one by one, wrapped up in the gentle dark.

The end. Sleep well. 🌙
