# new-school

## Pipeline (read-aloud script)

Leo and the Door That Was Too Big

Leo lived in a small town, where the bakery on the corner filled the whole street with the smell of warm bread. Tomorrow was an enormous day. Tomorrow, Leo would start at Maple Hill School. The school had a tall blue door, and Leo had never once walked through it.

That evening, his tummy felt crowded with fluttering moths. [whisper] Every time he pictured the new classroom, the moths beat their wings faster.

Where would he sit? Who would he talk to? What if nobody talked to him at all?

"I am not nervous," Leo told the kitchen clock, very loudly. "Not one bit. Not even a tiny bit."

But his voice wobbled like jelly on a plate, and the moths flapped harder than ever.

Leo gave up. He trudged across the hall and sat down on the bottom stair, his chin resting on his knees.

A rustle came from under the coat rack. Out tottered Pim, a shy hedgehog from the garden, with a leaf stuck to her prickles. She climbed up and sat beside him.

"Hic," said Pim.

"Are you all right?" asked Leo.

"Hic. I hiccup when I'm worried," said Pim. "And I am worried. About school. About tomorrow."

Leo let out a long, wobbly breath. "Me too," he admitted. "I'm nervous too."

Pim blinked her small black eyes. "But you said you weren't."

"I say that," said Leo. "The moths don't agree."

"Moths?"

"In my tummy. Hundreds of them." [pause]

Pim giggled, then hiccupped again. "I've been practising a brave word all afternoon," she whispered. "In my den, under a big leaf."

"What is it?" asked Leo.

[whisper] Pim leaned close. "Hello."

"Hello," said Leo. It came out as small as a squeak.

"Better!" said Pim. "But first, breathe in slowly. The word is much easier to say after a slow breath. Hic. Try."

So Leo breathed in slowly through his nose. He let the breath drift out, [softly] and then he said "Hello" to the stair rail. The moths settled, just a tiny bit.

"Pim," said Leo, "what if the school is too big? What if I get lost in all those corridors?"

Pim thought hard, and her nose twitched. "Then we go one step at a time," she said. "That's what my granny always says. Let's practise. Breathe in for four, and out for four."

[together: One step, then breathe, then the next step.]

They breathed together. In for one, two, three, four. Out for one, two, three, four. The moths slowed their flapping. Some even stopped to sit down and rest.

Leo thought about it. He did not have to be brave all at once. He only had to be brave for one small hello at a time.

"Tomorrow," he said, "I'll say hello to the first person who looks up at me. And that will be enough."

"That's a very good plan," said Pim. "Hic. Almost no hiccups at all."

Outside, the bakery lights dimmed, one by one. The moths folded their wings and lay down in Leo's tummy, quiet as paper cranes. [yawn] Leo carried his new school bag to the door and set it down where he could see it from his bed.

Then he climbed under his blanket and hugged his soft bear tight. His breathing grew slow and easy, in and out, one step, then breathe, then the next step.

[together: One step, then breathe, then the next step.]

Far away, the blue door at Maple Hill School waited in his mind. But it did not look so enormous now. It looked smaller and friendlier, like a door that might swing open with a gentle push.

Leo's eyes grew heavy. The warm bread smell drifted in through the window. Good night, Leo.

## Baseline (original single call)

That's a big day for both of you. New starts are hard, and it makes sense he's nervous. Most kids feel that way, and it doesn't mean the school is wrong for him.

A few things that often help the night before:

- **Let him talk it through.** Ask what he's most worried about. Sometimes naming the fear makes it smaller, and you'll learn what he needs.
- **Plan one small thing he can count on.** Knowing where his first class is, what the lunch routine looks like, or that you'll pick him up at a set time can take a lot of pressure off.
- **Give him a simple goal.** "Say hi to one person" or "find out one thing about a classmate" is more manageable than "make friends."
- **Keep your own energy calm.** Kids pick up on parents' anxiety, so a matter-of-fact "you'll be fine, and I'll see you after school" can go a long way.

Is he worried more about making friends, the schoolwork, or just the unfamiliar place?
